# Two short hinge pins that wait in the towers while the lid is put on

The hinge axis lies inside the lid, so the lid's own wall closes it at both ends, whatever the opening angle: the wall begins 16.8 mm from the middle, and beside a hinge tower a pin of 2 mm has 4.9 mm of room. A pin that passes through both ribs of the lid cannot be put in at any length. The single pin of 2 x 20 of revisions one to five could not have been mounted, and neither could the 2 x 18 the owner asked about.

The hinge therefore has two dowel pins of 2 x 8. Each is put into its tower from outside until it is flush with the tower's inner face, and waits there, standing 5.8 mm out. The lid is held about 65° open and slid forwards onto the hinge; then both pins are pushed in. Each rib of the lid turns on one pin over its full 3.4 mm, and the inner ends of the pins meet the spring sleeve that lies between the ribs.

Beside the knuckles the inside of the lid wall is relieved to 1.3 mm, from the rim to 2.5 mm above the axis and up to 16.5 mm from the middle, so that the waiting pins have room. Nothing shows outside.

Consequences: the winding direction of the spring has to be checked before the pins go in, and to take the lid off again, the spring and the printed sleeve inside it are cut and replaced. Once the pins hold the coil of the spring on the hinge axis, its forward leg can only turn about that axis. On the list of material to buy, two pins of 2 x 8 take the place of the one of 2 x 20.

Alternatives: one pin of 2 x 18 through a hole of 2.2 mm in each flank of the lid, which the owner declined; each hole would show as an oval of about 2 x 5 mm, 7 mm above the band. Ribs that are open on one side and clip onto a pin in the towers were examined and not proposed: an open knuckle would carry the blow of the lid on its stop, across the print layers, and a lid that comes off while it jumps open is the worst failure this device can have.

Origin: owner decision of revision six among alternatives worked out by the agent (research/06_mechanics_printing_ring.md, section 3). What holds the pins, how they are pushed in and where the spring's forward leg rests are conclusions of their own.
