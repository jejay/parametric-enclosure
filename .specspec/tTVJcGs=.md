# The ring pawl lies behind the slot: its finger crosses the slot under the deck, its knob stands behind the middle of the ring

The ring pawl is a straight bar under the deck, parallel to the slot and behind it. Its pivot lies 19 mm to the side, beyond the end of the walls of the slot. From there an arm reaches to the middle of the slot, and a tail of 12 mm goes the other way. At the end of the arm a finger of 4.4 x 4.3 mm turns forwards. It is a piece of a ring of 20.4 mm radius about the pivot, so that it slides in its own track: locked, it passes through a window in the rear wall of the slot, across the slot and through the ring, and 1.65 mm into a window of the front wall. It crosses the slot 21° off square.

What holds the ring: the finger lies directly under the deck. A pull at the ring presses it against the underside of the deck on both sides of the slot, on 9 and 7 mm², so the load passes neither through the arm nor through the pivot. Its two lower edges are sloped by 1.9 mm, because the inside of a ring is round: a ring of size 49 with a band up to 4.2 mm high stands under it. Under the windows the walls of the slot are closed.

The slot is 11.2 mm deep in its middle (2.4 mm of deck, 0.3 mm of play, 4.3 mm of finger, 4.2 mm under it) and 24.8 mm long at the surface of the deck. Below the deck it has walls of 2.0 mm and a floor of 1.6 mm, together a segment of a disc; in the print, which stands upside down, it closes like a dome and the bottom of the slot is a bridge of 3.1 mm. The ring of the drawing stands 2.6 mm lower than in revision nine.

The knob is a post of 5 mm on the arm, 7.5 mm behind the plane of the ring and in line with its middle, and stands 4.5 mm above the deck. Pulled 6 mm back, away from the ring (18°), it has the finger out of the slot. A spring on the tail, 10 mm from the pivot, takes it back until the tail rests on a post under the deck; uncut, the spring is 12.5 mm long there, 9.5 mm when the ring is free and 8.5 mm at the end of the slit.

Room: finger and knob have 1.0 mm on each side in the windows and in the slit. The pivot has 0.25 mm of play and two printed surfaces may each lie 0.2 mm off; moved by all of that together, in eight directions, the pawl touches nothing on its whole way.

The slit is longer than the lock needs, 25°. The pawl can only be brought up onto its boss with its finger behind the rear wall of the slot, which it is from 21° on. In use the finger therefore leaves the rear window over the last degrees and has to find it again; its end is tapered to 2 mm for that.

The pivot is that of the latch pawl: a boss of 5.0 mm in a bore of 5.5 mm, 0.3 mm of play along it, an M2 x 6 screw. The pawl is 4.3 mm thick, 0.7 mm more than the latch pawl, so that the hole in the boss can be 6.0 mm deep, enough for that screw under a thin washer, and still leave 1.0 mm of deck over it. A standard M2 washer is 5.0 mm across: by the drawn sizes it is narrower than the bore and no wider than the boss, and cannot hold a pawl. An M3 washer of 7 mm between it and the pawl does. That washer is an addition of the agent to the hardware the owner named; the boss takes the screw under the M2 washer alone as well.

Limits: geometry only. The strength of the spring and its solid length, taken as 5 mm, are not known, and whether a jolt can turn the pawl against the spring was not examined; only that the load of a pulled ring does not turn it. The bare ring of the drawing can be lifted 2.7 mm before it meets the finger; what the owner puts under the ring takes from that, and the 4.2 mm are the room for band and padding together. Nothing of this has been printed.

Alternatives: a pivot straight behind the ring, with a finger that crosses the slot 40° off square and needs wider windows. A knob with a cap wider than its slit, which cannot be put through the deck from below. A finger thinner than the pawl, to sink the ring less; its underside would not lie on the print bed. A blind pocket in the front wall instead of a window.

Origin: agent conclusion of 2026-11-25 for the owner's ring pawl, measured in the model (checks.py, section Ring lock; research/06_mechanics_printing_ring.md, section 3).
