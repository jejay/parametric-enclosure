# The ring pawl turns about a pivot in the plane of the ring; knob and spring sit on its tail

The pivot of the ring pawl lies in the plane of the ring, 20 mm from the middle of the ring, beyond the end of the slot. The finger of 4.4 x 4.3 mm is a piece of a ring of 20 mm radius about that pivot. It therefore slides in its own track and crosses the slot square: where it crosses, its middle lies 0.03 mm beside the middle of the ring, and the windows in the two walls of the slot reach from -3.2 to +3.6 mm. The pivot cannot lie nearer, because its hub has to pass the end of the slot's walls. The arm leaves the hub towards the back and runs behind the rear wall of the slot to its middle, where it carries the finger. Locked, the finger passes through the window of the rear wall, across the slot and through the ring, and 1.65 mm into the window of the front wall, and the arm rests against a pad on the rear wall.

What holds the ring: the finger lies directly under the deck. A pull at the ring presses it against the underside of the deck on both sides of the slot, on 9 and 6 mm², so the load passes neither through the arm nor through the pivot. Its two lower edges are sloped by 1.9 mm, because the inside of a ring is round. Under the windows the walls of the slot are closed.

The slot is 13.0 mm deep in its middle: 2.4 mm of deck, 0.3 mm of play, 4.3 mm of finger and 6.0 mm under the finger. It is a half-round bottom with 0.5 mm of upright wall above it and 25.0 mm long at the surface of the deck. Below the deck it has walls of 2.0 mm and a floor of 1.6 mm, together a segment of a disc; in the print, which stands upside down, it closes like a dome and the bottom of the slot is a bridge of 3.1 mm. The ring of the drawing, 19.2 mm across, stands in it with 68 % of its height below the deck and can be lifted 4.5 mm before its band meets the finger.

The pawl is kept slim: the arm is 4 mm wide and the tail is a bar of 6 mm from the hub to the knob. The tail points to the front left, 20° off the forward direction. The knob, a post of 5 mm that stands 4.5 mm above the deck, sits at its end, 14.9 mm from the pivot and 29 mm from the middle of the ring. Pulled 4.4 mm towards the middle line of the deck (17°) it has the finger out of the slot. Why there: further to the side the knob comes near the wall of the lid, which here keeps 4.2 mm from it and 3.2 mm when the lid is grown by 0.2 mm and stands askew; and in this direction the cup of the spring finds room between the tail and the servo.

The spring bears on a flat of the tail beside the knob, 15.2 mm from the pivot. Its cup under the deck is turned so that it opens along the way this point goes from locked to free; the end of the spring then leaves the axis of the cup by 0.17 mm at most. Uncut, the spring is 12.5 mm long when the pawl is locked, 8.0 mm when the ring is free and 5.9 mm at the end of the slit.

The pawl is not balanced, and has no counterweight. It weighs 1.68 g and its centre of mass lies 4.1 mm beside the pivot, 2.3 mm forwards and 3.4 mm towards the finger. By a hand estimate a blow of about 220 g on the back right of the ball starts to turn it towards free against a spring that holds with 1 N; a blow on the opposite side presses the arm onto its pad. A hard drop is taken to give 500 to 2000 g, so a hard knock on that side can open the pawl for an instant, and the ring would in that instant have to be thrown 13 mm upwards out of its slot, against what it is bedded in. The owner accepts this rather than a steel pin that could fall out among the electronics.

Room: finger and knob have 1.0 mm on each side in the windows and in the slit. The pivot has 0.25 mm of play and two printed surfaces may each lie 0.2 mm off; moved by all of that together, in eight directions, the pawl touches nothing on its whole way.

The slit is longer than the lock needs, 25°. The pawl can only be brought up onto its boss with its finger behind the rear wall of the slot, which it is from 20° on. In use the finger therefore leaves the rear window over the last degrees and has to find it again; its end is tapered to 2 mm for that.

The pivot is that of the latch pawl: a boss of 5.0 mm in a bore of 5.5 mm, 0.3 mm of play along it, an M2 x 6 screw. The pawl is 4.3 mm thick, 0.7 mm more than the latch pawl, so that the hole in the boss can be 6.0 mm deep, enough for that screw under a thin washer, and still leave 1.0 mm of deck over it. A standard M2 washer is 5.0 mm across: by the drawn sizes it is narrower than the bore and no wider than the boss, and cannot hold a pawl. An M3 washer of 7 mm between it and the pawl does. That washer is an addition of the agent to the hardware the owner named; the boss takes the screw under the M2 washer alone as well.

Limits: geometry only. The strength of the spring and its solid length, taken as 5 mm, are not known: at the end of the slit the spring would be 0.9 mm longer than that, and a spring that is solid sooner stops the knob sooner, which is why the pawl goes onto its boss before the spring goes in. What a blow does to the pawl is a hand estimate for a pawl printed solid. Nothing of this has been printed.

Alternatives: a counterweight, built into revisions eleven and twelve: a steel dowel pin of 4 x 8 mm in a round beside the hub, 7.0 mm from the pivot, which brings the centre of mass onto the pivot (about 1900 g instead of 220 g); it is held by a press fit or glue alone from falling, and the owner decided against it. RPAWL_PIN in params.py puts it back. The tail as a filled plate that spans hub, knob and pin, built into revision eleven: 2.24 g, and bulky in the owner's eyes. The first layout, built into revision ten: a straight bar behind the slot with its pivot 19 mm to the side, the finger crossing 21° off square, the knob behind the middle of the ring, the spring pressed from 12.5 to 9.5 mm, and the centre of mass 9.1 mm beside the pivot. A tail further to the side, where a knob of this height comes within 2 mm of the lid. The counterweight pin on the middle line of the tail, where it does not balance the pawl. The pin as the knob: one part less, but the knob would be a steel pin of 4 mm that is held by a press fit alone. A knob with a cap wider than its slit, which cannot be put through the deck from below.

Origin: agent conclusion of 2026-11-25 for the owner's ring pawl: second layout after the owner's second round of the same day, slimmed in a third, and without its counterweight by the owner's final decision; measured in the model (checks.py, section Ring lock; research/06_mechanics_printing_ring.md, section 1.6).
