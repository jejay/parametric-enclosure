# The feet of the pin under the spring leg are 3 mm thick and have 1.5 mm before and 1.3 mm behind its hole

The pin under the spring's forward leg is pressed into level holes in the feet of the two hinge towers, and the stage is printed in level layers. A pin that is pressed into such a hole pushes its two halves apart across the layers and tears at the foot in the plane through the axis of the hole. That plane is where a foot needs its plastic.

Each foot is 4.8 mm wide and, round the hole, 3.0 mm thick: 1.0 mm thicker than its tower, towards the middle of the hinge. The hole has 1.5 mm of foot before it, 1.3 mm behind it, towards the hinge, and 1.2 mm under it; in the plane through its axis that is 4.3 mm² before the hole and 3.7 mm² behind it, 8.0 mm² for a foot. The hole of 2.05 mm holds the pin in the thickness of the tower, 1.8 mm of pin in each foot, as before. In the added millimetre the hole is 2.3 mm wide: that part is not pressed on, it shares what the pressed part has to hold, and it leads the tip of the pin into the second foot. Above the added thickness a slope of 45° carries it in the print, which stands upside down.

Why not more: the owner asked for a little more material and no more, and behind the feet the stop tabs of the lid pass. The lid keeps 1.66 mm from the feet as drawn, where 0.5 mm are the rule. With all the room its hinge gives it at once, askew by 1.75° and moved 0.225 mm across its pins towards the feet, and grown by 0.2 mm, it keeps 1.02 mm; with the feet 0.2 mm fat as well, 0.75 mm. The corner of the stop tab that comes nearest lies 0.4 mm above the bed when the lid is printed, where a bulge of the first layer would take from this. Where the back face of the feet lies is the owner's choice among three: 1.5 mm of wall behind the hole left the lid 1.47, 0.82 and 0.56 mm in these three cases; 1.1 mm, the wall of the foot that broke, 1.86, 1.21 and 0.95 mm; the owner took the one between. On the outside the feet keep their faces, because the pin ends 0.2 mm inside them and is pushed in until it stands out of neither foot.

Limits: by a hand estimate for a ring round a rigid pin, the same fit puts a tenth to two fifths less stress across that plane behind the hole than before, and a fifth to almost a half less before it; the strain at the surface of the hole stays what the fit makes it. More plastic does not make a fit safe that is too tight, and how tight a level hole of 2.05 mm comes out depends on the printer. The wall of the test piece for the hinge pins has a level hole of this size in 2.0 mm of wall; the manual has the pin tried in it before the stage is printed and warns against force, and YOKE_HOLE is the value to adjust. Inside the stage a straight tool has 20 mm beside a foot, in line with the holes, so a hole that is too tight cannot be drilled open afterwards; a pin that sits loosely is glued. The feet as they are now have not been printed.

Alternatives: the added thickness on the outside, which would leave the ends of the pin 1 mm deep in their holes. The full 3 mm as a press fit, which would hold the pin over a longer length but press on all of the foot again. A foot that is longer below the hole, which adds nothing in the plane in which the foot split. A wider hole with glue from the start, which changes the fit; the owner asked for more material.

Origin: owner request of 2026-11-25, after a foot of the printed stage of revision eight had split on both sides of its hole, half of the hole in the piece that came off. That foot was 4.3 x 2.0 mm, with 1.1 mm beside the hole and 4.5 mm² in that plane. That the split followed the layers and came from the pressed-in pin is the agent's reading of the owner's description; the 3 N of the spring are 0.5 MPa on that section. Thickness, the wider hole and the front of the foot are agent conclusions; where its back face lies is the owner's decision of the same day, taken after asking what room sloppy prints leave the lid. All figures are measured in the model (research/06_mechanics_printing_ring.md, section 3).
