# The towers enclose the hinge pins; the spring sleeve keeps them

Each hinge tower encloses its pin all round. Its knuckle is a closed ring of 2.7 mm radius round the pin, with at least 1.6 mm of wall at every angle. The hinge axis lies almost on the sphere of the stage's dome, so half of that ring stands out of the dome, into the room that the thinned wall of the lid leaves behind the hinge. The ring is a solid of revolution about the hinge axis and therefore keeps the same 0.51 mm from that wall at every opening angle. A roof at 45° carries the part of the ring that would hang in the air when the stage is printed upside down.

The pins slide in the towers (bore 2.15 mm, 2.2 mm of tower) and are pressed into the spring sleeve. Each end of the sleeve has a blind hole of 1.95 mm, 1.8 mm deep, whose first 0.45 mm are a funnel, and a mounted pin stands on the floor of its hole. The sleeve fills the gap between the two ribs of the lid but for 0.15 mm at each end and does not pass their bores. A pin can therefore only come out by being pulled out of the sleeve, and the 2.7 mm of solid sleeve between the two holes stop it on its way in.

Why the press fit is in the sleeve and not in the towers: the sleeve prints with its holes upright and its layers round the pin, weighs 0.1 g, and its fit can be tried on a test piece and the sleeve printed again in minutes. In a tower the fit would sit in the wall behind the pin, which the stage prints across its layers, and trying it would mean printing the stage. Pins that slide in the towers can also be pushed through the ribs without force; force is needed only for the last 1.35 mm, and by then the ends of the pins lie where ordinary pliers have room.

How the pins go in: a small screwdriver behind the end of each waiting pin pushes it 3.6 mm, through its rib; the lid then hangs on the pins and the sleeve is moved onto their tips. With the lid opened as far as it goes, long-nose pliers press both pins into the sleeve until they stand on the floors of their holes. Their outer ends then lie a quarter of a millimetre outside the towers, so that a pin that is a little short is pressed home as well.

Limits: the press fit is friction and nothing else. A pin that worked loose could slide back to where it waited, and would then have left its rib. How firmly a pin holds depends on the printer; a test piece with holes of both fits is printed before stage and sleeve, and HINGE_PRESS and HINGE_SLIDE are the two values to adjust.

Alternatives: a press fit in the towers, as first drawn in revision six, for the reasons above; the pliers would also have had to press from behind the waiting pins, where the lid leaves 1.5 mm for a jaw. Towers left open at the back, with the pins put into the ribs first and a keeper behind each pin: the lid would drop in and come off again without cutting the sleeve, but a keeper finds neither room to be pushed in, 2 to 4 mm behind the towers at any angle of the lid, nor plastic to be anchored in. A hinge axis 2.9 mm further forwards, so that a whole knuckle lies inside the dome: the pocket in the band would take 4.9 of its 7 mm.

Origin: agent conclusion, worked out with the owner on 2026-11-24 after the owner's first print. It showed that the towers of revision six did not enclose the pins: the dome of the stage had cut each knuckle in half, bore included (research/06_mechanics_printing_ring.md, section 3).
