# Only slopes lead the hook into its slot and out of it

The hook of the lid can come down, or go up, as far as 2.9 mm beside its place. On its way it may be pushed aside by slopes and guided by walls that stand along its path, and it may push the pawl. It must never meet a ledge or a ceiling.

Coming down: the slot through the deck has a funnel on both sides, 2.3 mm beside the tooth and 3.0 mm beside the stem, which takes a hook 3.5 mm off. Beside the stem the deck is thickened by a rib, so that the funnel can be that deep and an upright wall remains below it that guides the stem at 0.5 mm. The top of the pawl's nose slopes at 45° towards the stem, and so far from it that a stem as far off as the hinge allows lands on the slope and not on the flat behind it. The underside of the tooth is a slope by itself and pushes the pawl aside.

Going up: beside the tooth the edge of the slot under the deck is sloped, 2 mm wide and 1.5 mm high, and beside the slot the inside of the dome is hollowed 2.2 mm for the front end of the tooth, because the dome closes in above a tooth that rises beside its place. A square edge and that dome were a ceiling, the one place where a lid could have been caught for good.

The hook moves in an arc about the hinge. When its lower end passes the pawl it stands about a millimetre further back than when the lid is shut. The flank of the pawl behind its nose therefore keeps out of the way of the stem at every angle of the pawl, and the sloped edge of the nose runs on behind the nose. In every revision before, the stem of a centred lid came down on a flat corner of the pawl there, 0.5 mm wide, with the lid 5° from shut: closing the lid had never been followed in the model, only opening it.

How it is tested: the lid is moved in steps of 0.05°, with its hook grown by 0.2 mm and starting up to 2.9 mm beside its place. Where the hook meets deck or pawl it may be pushed towards the middle, or the pawl turned, but in one step never further than twice the way the hook travelled in that step. A slope passes that test; a ledge asks for more at once and counts as stuck.

Limits: that rule knows nothing of friction, and the hook is moved sideways as a whole, not turned by the two degrees the hinge allows.

Alternatives: a slot so wide beside the tooth that the deck is never above it; a hook that came down there would land on the flat top of the pawl beyond its slope. Funnels that stand up from the deck; the deck is the face on which the stage is printed.
