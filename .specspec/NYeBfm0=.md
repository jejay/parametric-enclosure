# The tongue's rear end lies under the ball's belly; four screws from below hold it

The tongue is one block, 72 mm wide at the ball and 66 mm at its round tip, 15 mm high without the blades. It begins 10 mm in front of the ball's axis, under the belly of the ball, and reaches 70 mm beyond the ball; in revision three it was 76 mm wide and reached 76 mm. Nothing of it lies beside or behind the ball. Its rear end is hollowed to the shape of the ball, and its rear corners are cut off at 45°. Four countersunk M3 x 16 screws from below pull it onto four small pads round the screw holes; everywhere else it keeps 0.25 mm of air. The screws end in threaded inserts in four bosses low on the front wall of the bowl.

Why 15 mm: it lies within the owner's two to two and a half times, and it is what makes a tongue without a ring sound. Under the screws the tongue is 7 mm thick, where a thin tongue would have had a flange of a few millimetres; the bending stress in the tray under a push on the tip falls from about 13 MPa to about 1.5 MPa; the antenna rises to 11.7 mm above the table; and the hall switch gets the depth it needs. The price is length. The belly of the ball bulges over a tongue of this height, so the reader, and with it the place of the figure, lie 6 mm further forward than in revision two. That is also why the tongue cannot get much shorter: its length is the reader board plus the wall round it.

The underside of the tongue lies in the plane of the ball's flat, without any offset: ball and tongue stand on the table together. The four pads set this height. Printed parts differ by a tenth of a millimetre or two, so the manual says how a device that rocks is corrected at the pads.

Without the ring, the device stands on the flat of the ball and the underside of the tongue together. The centre of gravity stays at least 24 mm inside the edge of that area, with the lid closed or open and with a figure on the tongue; with the ring of revision two it was 41 mm. Behind its axis the ball has only its own flat of 34 mm to stand on.

The seat is part of a sphere, so its pressure carries no moment about the centre of the ball: a push on the tongue is held by the screws alone. By hand formula a push of 20 N on the tip, with the ball held, pulls on each front screw with at most 46 N. Not examined: what a fall onto the tongue does.

The tongue is two prints in the same green. The tray has the hollow rear end, the pocket for the reader, the channel for its wires and, round its top, a rim of 2 mm that frames the grass top. The grass top is one flat sheet of 1.6 mm with its tufts, so that its underside prints on the bed without bridging. It lies in the frame, 0.15 mm clear and level with the rim. The walls of the frame lean forwards by 1.2 mm over the thickness of the plate: the plate goes in forwards and down, and the rim at the tip then overhangs its front edge and holds it down. Four small screws from below hold its rear half; they end inside the roots of grass tufts in the two corners, and nothing shows on top. With the ball in place the plate cannot slide back out.

Why a frame: since revision four no grass stands at the tip, so no tuft can hide a screw there, and a plate that is held at its rear half only would lift at the tip. The price is a fine joint line on the top, 2 to 3 mm inside the edge; the side wall of the tongue has no joint any more.

The wires leave the pocket in a channel towards the ball and enter the bowl through a window low on its front wall, which the tongue covers. It lets a plug section of 15.5 x 6.5 mm pass, so the tongue can be fitted to the finished ball and taken off again.

Origin: agent conclusion.
