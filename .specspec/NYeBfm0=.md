# The tongue's rear end lies under the ball's belly; four screws from below hold it

The tongue is one block, 76 mm wide and 15 mm high without the blades. It begins 10 mm in front of the ball's axis, under the belly of the ball, and reaches 76 mm beyond the ball. Nothing of it lies beside or behind the ball. Its rear end is hollowed to the shape of the ball. Four countersunk M3 x 16 screws from below pull it onto four small pads round the screw holes; everywhere else it keeps 0.25 mm of air. The screws end in threaded inserts in four bosses low on the front wall of the bowl.

Why 15 mm: it lies within the owner's two to two and a half times, and it is what makes a tongue without a ring sound. Under the screws the tongue is 7 mm thick, where a thin tongue would have had a flange of a few millimetres; the bending stress in the tray under a push on the tip falls from about 13 MPa to about 1.3 MPa; the antenna rises to 11.7 mm above the table; and the hall switch gets the depth it needs. The price is length. The belly of the ball bulges over a tongue of this height, so the reader, and with it the standing spot, lie 7 mm further forward than in revision two.

The underside of the tongue lies in the plane of the ball's flat, without any offset: ball and tongue stand on the table together. The four pads set this height. Printed parts differ by a tenth of a millimetre or two, so the manual says how a device that rocks is corrected at the pads.

Without the ring, the device stands on the flat of the ball and the underside of the tongue together. The centre of gravity stays at least 27 mm inside the edge of that area, with the lid closed or open and with a figure on the spot; with the ring of revision two it was 41 mm. Behind its axis the ball has only its own flat of 34 mm to stand on.

The seat is part of a sphere, so its pressure carries no moment about the centre of the ball: a push on the tongue is held by the screws alone. By hand formula a push of 20 N on the tip, with the ball held, pulls on each front screw with at most 49 N. Not examined: what a fall onto the tongue does.

The tongue is two prints. The tray has the hollow rear end, the pocket for the reader and the channel for its wires. The turf is one flat sheet of 1.6 mm, so that its underside prints on the bed without bridging; 6 small screws from below hold it, they end inside the roots of grass tufts, and nothing shows on top. The rear corners of the tongue are cut off at 45°, so that the turf ends bluntly beside the ball and not in two thin points.

The wires leave the pocket in a channel towards the ball and enter the bowl through a window low on its front wall, which the tongue covers. It lets a plug section of 15.5 x 6.5 mm pass, so the tongue can be fitted to the finished ball and taken off again.
