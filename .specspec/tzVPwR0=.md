# Tufts thrown at random into the two corners beside the ball; a bare front end without a mark

The grass follows the tall grass of Pokémon Let's Go: tufts of broad, lance-shaped blades that fan out of one root. They are drawn a little broader than there, end in blunt tips of 1.2 x 1.0 mm and grow out of a low solid mound, because a single upright PETG blade of this size is estimated to break at about 14 N.

Only four places in the lawn are set by hand: one tuft has to stand on each of the four screws that hold the grass top, and those places are deliberately not mirrored. Everything else is drawn by a random generator with a fixed seed. Where a tuft lands is weighted: most likely near a point in each corner between the ball and a side of the tongue (x = 37, y = ±28 mm), less likely the further away, thinning out towards the centre line and never rooted within 8 mm of it; the two corners take turns. The size of a tuft is drawn after its place, again and again until one fits: the height of its main blade between 7 and 21 mm, its two to seven blades (tall tufts tend to have more), the direction in which it spreads, how far its blades lean and curl, and how broad or slim its leaves are. A tall tuft does not stay where the belly of the ball would crop it, so the grass is low next to the ball and taller further out. Tufts may run into each other, as in a thicket, but none has a twin on the other side and no blade leans out over the edge of the tongue. The seed is a parameter; another seed gives another lawn.

No grass stands beside or in front of a figure: every tuft is rooted behind a line 10 mm behind the centre of the figure and at least 8 mm from a foot of 45 mm. That is the space the tongue gives up: without grass round the figure it narrows to the figure, and since revision five it ends just in front of the reader board.

The place of the figure carries no mark, by the owner's decision. The front end of the tongue is the only cue: the foot of a centred figure ends 2 mm short of the front edge and leaves 10.5 mm of ground on each side. Reader and hall switch are expected to tolerate about 8 mm; a figure put down by eye can stand further off, most easily too far back. Offered and not taken: the disc of revisions two and three in the same green, set off by another top-layer pattern.

A magnet or a steel disc under the spot would give the feel of a Toniebox. Both were ruled out: they would hold the figure, and the hall switch sits there.

Tongue and grass are one light green. Turf plate and tufts are therefore one body and one print in one colour; revisions two and three had kept them as three bodies for two greens.
