# Tufts of broad blades thrown at random, a level spot in the brighter green, an open front

The grass follows the tall grass of Pokémon Let's Go: tufts of broad, lance-shaped blades that fan out of one root. They are drawn a little broader than there, end in blunt tips of 1.2 x 1.0 mm and grow out of a low solid mound, because a single upright PETG blade of this size is estimated to break at about 14 N.

Only 6 places in the lawn are set by hand: one tuft has to stand on each of the 6 screws that hold the turf, and those places are deliberately not mirrored. Everything else is drawn by a random generator with a fixed seed: the place of every further tuft, the height of its main blade between 7 and 21 mm, its two to seven blades (tall tufts tend to have more), the direction in which it spreads, how far its blades lean and curl, and how broad or slim its leaves are. The largest tufts are placed first, so that they still find room. No tuft is placed opposite another one, a tall tuft does not go where the belly of the ball would crop it, and no blade leans out over the edge of the tongue. The seed is a parameter; another seed gives another lawn.

The standing spot is marked by colour only: a disc in the bright green of the blades, 50 mm across, 1 mm deep and level with the dark green ground. Within 30.0 mm of its centre nothing rises above the surface and nothing is recessed. The nearest grass is 8.3 mm from a foot of 45 mm, 2 mm more than in revision two and at the cost of grass, as the owner allowed. No grass stands within 45° of straight ahead: the figure visibly stands free and is put down from the front.

A magnet or a steel disc under the spot would give the feel of a Toniebox. Both were ruled out: they would hold the figure, and the hall switch sits there.

Why mark the spot at all: the tag has to stand over the antenna. A foot that covers the disc is centred within 2.5 mm; reader and hall switch are expected to tolerate about 8 mm.

The ground is one darker green; the blades and the disc are one brighter green. The turf is therefore the one part of the device that is printed in two colours.
