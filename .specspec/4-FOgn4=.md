# The ring stands in a slot of the stage, bedded in paper or cloth, and a pawl moved by hand passes through it

The ring stands upright, facing the front, in a slot that is cut into the stage itself: 3.1 mm wide, with a round bottom of 25 mm diameter, 13 mm deep. The slot is wider than the ring on purpose. The owner fills what the ring leaves free with paper or cloth, which holds the ring firm and keeps it from being scratched.

A pawl locks the ring. It is a modified copy of the latch pawl: it turns under the deck on the same kind of pivot, with the same screw and steel washer, and a spring holds it locked. Its tip passes into the slot and through the ring, as the steel pin did before, in the middle of the ring. It is moved by hand only; no servo acts on it. Slit and windows leave generous room round the pawl.

Its knob stands up through a curved slit that is left out of the deck, and it stands on the tail of the pawl, not behind the ring: a finger does not pass through a ring that stands in its slot, and from behind the ring is in the way of the knob. The knob is then pulled where it would have been pushed. The tail is a little longer than it was, and the compression spring of 4 x 15 mm, uncut, is used harder, from about 12.5 mm when locked to about 8 mm when the ring is free, so that the lock is harder to work. The cup of the spring may be turned so that it opens along the way of the tail. The pawl carries no counterweight pin: the owner does not want the risk that a pin falls out one day.

What it replaces: an exchangeable cradle in a pocket of the deck with a felt-lined slot, and a 3 mm steel pin with a printed slider pressed onto it (revisions one to nine). The owner built it and called it a horrible fail: the slider could not be fixed on the pin, also not with glue. The latch pawl of the same build worked really well, which is why the lock of the ring is now a pawl too.

Origin: owner decisions of 2026-11-25, in two rounds. First round: the slot in the stage, 3.1 mm wide and 25 mm in diameter (first written as a radius), 4.2 mm under the tip of the pawl, which made the slot 11.2 mm deep; the pawl as a copy of the latch pawl with an M2 x 6 screw and a standard M2 steel washer and a 4 x 15 mm spring. Second round, after seeing the first layout: the knob on the tail, the longer tail and the harder use of the spring, the opening centred on the ring, and a deeper slot, 13 mm, with the remark that the room under the tip may grow. A counterweight pin was proposed by the agent, taken up by the owner with a maybe, built into revisions eleven and twelve, and taken out again by the owner's final decision of the same day. Where the pawl lies and how it is shaped is a conclusion of its own.
