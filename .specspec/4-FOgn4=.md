# The ring stands in a slot of the stage, bedded in paper or cloth, and a pawl moved by hand passes through it

The ring stands upright, facing the front, in a slot that is cut into the stage itself: 3.1 mm wide, with a round bottom of 25 mm diameter. The slot is wider than the ring on purpose. The owner fills what the ring leaves free with paper or cloth, which holds the ring firm and keeps it from being scratched.

A pawl locks the ring. It is a modified copy of the latch pawl: it turns under the deck on the same kind of pivot, with the same screw and steel washer, and a spring holds it locked. Its tip passes into the slot and through the ring, as the steel pin did before, and leaves 4.2 mm under it for the profile height of the ring. It is a little larger than the latch pawl in radius and in reach, so that a compression spring of 4 x 15 mm works in it uncut. Its knob stands up through a curved slit that is left out of the deck. It is moved by hand only; no servo acts on it. Slit and windows leave generous room round the pawl.

What it replaces: an exchangeable cradle in a pocket of the deck with a felt-lined slot, and a 3 mm steel pin with a printed slider pressed onto it (revisions one to nine). The owner built it and called it a horrible fail: the slider could not be fixed on the pin, also not with glue. The latch pawl of the same build worked really well, which is why the lock of the ring is now a pawl too.

Origin: owner decision of 2026-11-25, with the owner's three dimensions of the slot and the owner's hardware: an M2 x 6 screw with a standard M2 steel washer, as on the latch pawl of the build, and a 4 x 15 mm spring. The 25 mm were first written as a radius and confirmed as a diameter on the same day. Where the pawl lies and how it is shaped is a conclusion of its own.
