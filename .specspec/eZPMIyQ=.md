# The pawl swings 44° and lies 0.75 mm above the tooth

The released pawl has to be out of the way of a hook that stands beside its place, also when the servo stops short. It turns 44°, where 14° would free a centred hook, and could turn 54° before it meets anything. Released, it lets a hook pass that stands 6 mm towards it; with a servo 4° short, which turns it by 36°, still 3 mm. While the lid is shut the collar keeps the hook within 1.5 mm of its place.

The nose of the pawl covers the outer 1.5 mm of the tooth and keeps 1.3 mm from the stem of the hook. At rest it still holds a hook that stands 1.8 mm away from it; the wall of the slot lets the hook go 0.5 mm.

Along its pivot the pawl has 0.3 mm of play between the deck and its washer, and 0.25 mm round the boss. The underside of the nose lies 0.75 mm above the tooth when the pawl is pushed up and 0.45 mm when it lies on its washer, and a slope of 0.5 mm under its edge lifts a pawl that lies low. So the pawl comes back over a tooth that stands half a millimetre high, by its print or because the lid does not lie quite down. Lid, band, stage and pawl are four prints stacked on each other.

Consequences: a latched lid is held 0.75 mm above its seat at the hook by its own spring and can be pressed down by a little less than a millimetre at the front. The arm of the servo sweeps from 35° on one side of straight ahead to 6° on the other. The paper clip of the emergency opening meets the pawl after 16 mm and is pushed 5 mm further. The return spring is a 12 mm piece of ball-pen spring whose end sits in a cup under the deck; with the pawl at 54° it is still 1.2 mm longer than solid.

What was there before: a pawl that turned 24° and then cleared the tooth by 0.4 mm, and 0.15 mm between nose and tooth in a model that let the pawl float 1.1 mm along its pivot.

Alternatives: a latch face that slopes, so that the pawl takes up the gap by itself; over the 1.5 mm it covers, a slope that still holds takes up 0.15 mm. Pawls of several heights to choose from when the device is put together; not needed with this gap. A thinner nose that lies lower; it would break under a pull on the lid that this one stands.

Limits: whether the pawl turns freely on its printed boss and is brought back by its spring shows on the printed parts only; the manual has it tried before the stage is built in. Shock that could move the pawl is not analysed.
