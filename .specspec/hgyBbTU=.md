# What the verification covers, and what it cannot

The checks establish: each printed part is one valid, watertight solid; no two of the 40 items overlap in the closed assembly except declared threads and press fits; critical clearances hold; the lid passes through its travel in 2.5° steps without contact and is stopped at the end; the latch holds when engaged and frees the lid when released; pawl, servo arm and lock slider move freely; the ring is retained when locked and free when released; the emergency path is unobstructed; core, band and stage can be put together in the order of the manual; the ball is self-righting, stands with the lid open, and the spring beats gravity. For the grass tongue they establish: the ball rests on four pads and can be lowered onto the tongue; the underside of the tongue lies in the plane of the ball's flat; reader, hall switch and grass top go together, and the rim of the tray holds the tip of the grass top; a plug passes the hidden cable window; each of the four Pokémon figures on the tongue clears the ball; the standing surface is plain and unmarked, with all grass behind the figure; the grass is thin, stands in the two corners beside the ball and has no twins left and right; the turf screws end inside tuft roots; and the device stands on flat and tongue with the centre of gravity at least 24 mm inside the edge.

They do not establish that a Tonie's tag is reached from 3 mm below its foot, or with metal under the tongue, or that its magnets are those the hall switch is laid out for, or that a figure is put down close enough to the unmarked spot, that the printed parts have these dimensions, that the spring delivers its catalogue torque, or how the speaker sounds. Passing checks are evidence about geometry only.
