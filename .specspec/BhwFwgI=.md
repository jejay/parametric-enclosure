# The seat of the spring's forward leg is a wire that goes in after the pins

The forward leg of the lid spring rests on a steel wire of about 1 mm that passes through holes of 1.4 mm in the feet of the two hinge towers, about 12 mm in front of the hinge axis. The wire goes in last. With the pins in and the lid at its stop, the leg lies on the inner edge of the lid's rim, 8.5° below its seat; it is lifted with a screwdriver and the wire is pushed through under it, from the side, inside the stage. One end of the wire is bent over beforehand and stops it at the foot; a drop of glue fixes that end.

Why the seat is not printed: once the pins hold the coil on the hinge axis, the leg can only turn about that axis, and a relaxed leg lies below its seat. A fixed seat then lies in the leg's own way; the printed yoke of revisions one to six could not have been reached. Mounting the spring already wound would mean holding it against its torque while the pins find the sleeve. A seat that is put in afterwards needs neither, and steel does not give way under the 3 N that rest on it for weeks.

Consequences: 23 mm of steel wire of 0.8 to 1.2 mm join the list of parts; a straightened paper clip does. The legs of the spring are cut to 14 mm, which the manual now says. The wire has to come out before the lid can be taken off, and taking the lid off costs the spring as well as the sleeve, because the sleeve can only be cut through the coil.

Alternatives: a printed seat with a gap beside it, past which the leg is sprung sideways; it needs supports under the seat in the print and holds the leg only by a lip. A seat lower down, which the leg would reach by itself when the lid is fully open; the spring would then hold the open lid with 2 N mm instead of 7.
