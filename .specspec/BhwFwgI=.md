# The seat of the spring's forward leg is a pin that goes in after the hinge pins

The forward leg of the lid spring rests on a third dowel pin, 2 x 18 mm, that passes through holes of 2.05 mm in the feet of the two hinge towers, 12.5 mm in front of the hinge axis, and sits 1.8 mm in each. It goes in last. With the hinge pins in and the lid at its stop, the leg lies on the inner edge of the lid's rim, 8.5° below its seat; it is lifted with a screwdriver and the pin is pushed through under it, from the side, inside the stage. The holes hold the pin by a light press fit.

Why the seat is not printed: once the hinge pins hold the coil on the hinge axis, the leg can only turn about that axis, and a relaxed leg lies below its seat. A fixed seat then lies in the leg's own way; the printed yoke of revisions one to six could not have been reached. Mounting the spring already wound would mean holding it against its torque while the pins find the sleeve. A seat that is put in afterwards needs neither, and steel does not give way under the 3 N that rest on it for weeks.

Why a pin and no longer a wire: in revision seven the seat was a piece of wire of about 1 mm in holes of 1.4 mm, bent over at one end and glued. The owner has since bought dowel pins in many lengths, 2 x 18 among them. A pin is straight and of a known diameter and needs neither bending nor glue.

Consequences: a pin of 2 x 18 takes the place of the wire on the list of parts. The legs of the spring are cut to 14 mm. The pin has to be pushed out before the lid can be taken off, and taking the lid off costs the spring as well as the sleeve, because the sleeve can only be cut through the coil.

Alternatives: a printed seat with a gap beside it, past which the leg is sprung sideways; it needs supports under the seat in the print and holds the leg only by a lip. A seat lower down, which the leg would reach by itself when the lid is fully open; the spring would then hold the open lid with 2 N mm instead of 7.

Origin: agent conclusion, worked out with the owner on 2026-11-24; the pin later on the same day, after the owner had named the hardware now at hand (research/06_mechanics_printing_ring.md, section 3).
