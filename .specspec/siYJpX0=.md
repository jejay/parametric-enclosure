# The lid is guided near its seat and touches nothing from 8° on

How far the lid can be off follows from its bores. A pin of 2 mm has 0.3 mm of room in each rib of the lid and 0.15 mm in each hinge tower: the lid can stand 1.75° askew, about the vertical or about the fore-aft axis, and slide half a millimetre along its pins. That moves its rim beside the ball by up to 1.8 mm and the hook of the latch by up to 2.9 mm. A printed surface is taken to lie up to 0.2 mm beside the model.

Three rules follow. Every gap between the lid and what it passes is wider than two such surfaces can take up, 0.5 mm or more: 0.6 mm to the collar of the stage, 0.6 mm to the ring of the button, 0.5 mm between the ribs of the lid and the hinge towers, 0.5 mm beside the stem of the hook. Where the lid has to be led, onto its seat and with its hook into the slot, it is led by walls that stand along its path and by slopes, and these only widen as the lid rises. Everywhere else the parts keep clear of a lid that uses all its play.

The collar of the stage is the guide: 3 mm high, 0.6 mm inside the lip of the lid, with a slope of 45° above it. Beside the ball it holds the lip until the lid is 4° open, and by then the tooth of the hook stands inside its slot. It ends 100° from the front. Further back the lip swings inwards while it rises, and a wall there would stand in its way; there the stage is cut back with the solid of revolution that the lip sweeps about the hinge axis, taken for a lid that stands 1.8 mm to either side, and 0.6 mm more. The ring of the button gets its room on the bezel, which is 0.4 mm smaller from the rim of the bowl upwards, so that band, stage and lid, which all come down over it, pass it freely. The lid is not changed.

The result, for a lid grown by 0.2 mm: up to 8° of opening the hook positions in which the lid touches nothing widen from ±0.3 mm to the whole ±2.9 mm, and no position that is free at one angle is lost at a larger one; from 8° to the stop nothing touches the lid, however it stands. Tilted about the fore-aft axis, its lower side lies on the band, the seat it rests on anyway, until it has risen clear at about 30°. A lid grown by 0.4 mm still lies down without being wedged.

What was there before: 0.2 mm between the notch of the lid and the ring of the button, on flanks 12° from the vertical, where two fat surfaces wedge the lid in its seat and the spring lifts its front with half a newton; a collar of 0.35 mm that the lip approached by another 0.18 mm at its rear ends; 0.2 mm beside the hinge towers; and, just outside the old cut-back of the stage, a lip that passed at 0.1 to 0.3 mm.

Limits: the bores of the ribs, which give most of the play, and the pocket of the band, in which the rear rim of the lid runs with 0.4 mm, belong to parts that are printed and stay as they are. Where the stage sits in the band adds to or takes from that last gap; the manual has the stage pushed towards the button and the gap tried. Friction is not in the checks: a contact counts as harmless when it is a wall along the path or a slope. The 0.2 mm are an assumption about the owner's printer.

Alternatives: less play by tighter bores in the towers; they give the smaller part of it, and the pins have to slide through them when the lid is hinged. A collar that leans back, so that the lid comes free at once; the hook could then wander before it stands in its slot. Room for the ring of the button on the lid; the lid is printed.
